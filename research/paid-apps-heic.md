# How paid Windows photo apps decode HEIC

Research for issue #52 of the map #41 (decoding pictures without Magick.NET). Gathered 2026-11-25 and 26 from vendors' help pages, release notes, licence agreements and third-party notices, the patent pools' licensee lists, and forum answers. Patent facts are reported as vendors and pools state them; this is not legal advice. Where a page could not be read directly (Adobe, DxO and ACDSee block automated reading), the archived copy or a search engine's extract of it was used, and the table says so.

## Short answer

Of 26 commercial or widely used Windows photo apps and services looked at, there are four patterns:

1. **Windows' own codecs, and the person buys HEVC Video Extensions ($0.99): 10.** Photoshop, Bridge, Capture One, DxO PhotoLab, Mylio, Excire Foto, FastStone, IrfanView, Zoner Studio (for ordinary iPhone HEIC) and Microsoft's own Photos. Each vendor's help page tells the person to install HEIF Image Extensions and HEVC Video Extensions from the Microsoft Store, several naming the price. Capture One puts it plainly: HEIC is supported "to the extent that those are supported by the underlying operating system".
2. **A decoder bundled in the app: 5, plus one app's add-on.** ACDSee (libde265, LGPL-3.0, with DigiDNA's MIT ISOBMFF parser, named in its licence agreement), Lightroom, Lightroom Classic and Camera Raw (libde265, LGPL-3.0, in Adobe's notices; their help page names no Microsoft extension for HEIC), XnView MP (decoding "built in"; its developer has named libheif), CopyTrans HEIC and iMazing HEIC Converter (both run on Windows 7, which has no HEVC extension, so they must carry their own; inferred, not stated). Zoner offers a free **Zoner HEVC Codec** of its own for the variants Microsoft's does not decode (10-bit, 4:2:2, 4:4:4, HDR). Zoner is a licensee of both HEVC pools.
3. **Rendering in the cloud: 4.** OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Photos and iCloud show HEIC in the browser and in their apps on any Windows. iCloud.com and the iPhone itself can also hand over JPEGs instead. The desktop sync clients only move files; they decode nothing.
4. **Not documented: 7.** Affinity Photo, Luminar Neo, ON1 Photo RAW, PaintShop Pro, PhotoDirector, Photo Mechanic and Photoshop Elements (whose notices also name libde265) list HEIC as supported, but say nothing on how it is decoded on Windows.

**GPU decoding of HEIC stills:** none found. Lightroom Classic 15.5 needs hardware decoding for HEVC *video*, not for pictures.

**Paying on the person's behalf:** none found. No vendor buys or bundles the $0.99 extension for its users. Where a vendor carries a decoder, the app is free (CopyTrans for personal use, iMazing, Zoner's add-on) or the decoder is part of the paid licence (ACDSee, Lightroom, XnView MP's commercial licence). Zoner sends people to the Store for the ordinary case even though it holds a pool licence and makes a codec of its own.

**What buyers expect:** paying for a Windows photo app does not mean HEIC works out of the box. Most vendors document the Microsoft Store step as the buyer's job. Converters and free viewers compete on "no Store purchase needed" (XnView's HEIC page, CopyTrans). Trovolve's rule that nothing has to be bought is stricter than what paid apps do.

## Licensing statements

- **No vendor's public statement on HEVC patents was found**, either for or against supporting HEIC. The one reason given in writing is Capture One's: support goes "to the extent that those are supported by the underlying operating system". DxO forum members speculate about patent costs, but DxO staff have said nothing there.
- **Notices that name an HEVC decoder carry no HEVC patent notice.** ACDSee's 2024 agreement and Adobe's Lightroom Classic, Lightroom Desktop and Photoshop Elements notices name libde265 under the LGPL. Adobe's notices carry an MPEG LA *AVC* notice for MainConcept, but no HEVC notice was found in any notices read (Adobe's four, ACDSee's, Topaz's, iMazing's).
- **The pools:** Adobe is on HEVC Advance's licensee list ([Access Advance licensees](https://accessadvance.com/hevc-advance-patent-pool-licensees/), 430 names) but not on Via LA's. CyberLink, Zoner, Microsoft and Google are on both. ACD Systems, DigiDNA, CopyTrans, XnView, DxO, Capture One, Serif, Skylum, ON1, Corel, Mylio, Excire, Camera Bits, FastStone, IrfanView, Apple and Dropbox are on neither. Via LA's programme "extends to devices implementing the technology" and says "It is up to each user to determine its own licensing needs" ([Via LA HEVC/VVC](https://www.via-la.com/licensing-programs/hevc-vvc/)). Being absent from a list does not show that a vendor lacks a licence.
- **Dropped or added support because of licensing:** none found from these makers. Microsoft's own move, taking away the free HEVC extension and selling it for $0.99, is covered in #42. A forum report says that from 15.5 Lightroom Classic depends on a shared Adobe HEVC component for video; this is anecdotal ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/bug-reports-674/p-lrc-15-5-video-files-issues-windows-1635342)).

## What buyers expect

- People who buy a Windows photo app routinely meet a Microsoft Store step for HEIC, written into the vendor's own help (Adobe, Capture One, DxO, Excire, Mylio, Zoner, IrfanView), and many vendors name the price. It is presented as the PC's shortcoming, not the app's.
- The selling point of the apps that bundle a decoder is that no purchase is needed (XnView's HEIC page sets "Paid Store add-on" against "Free for private use"; CopyTrans runs on Windows 7). Those apps are viewers, converters and one cataloguer (ACDSee), and every one that names its decoder names libde265 or libheif (LGPL), which Trovolve's rules exclude.
- Apps that run in the cloud avoid the question by decoding on their servers, which Trovolve's privacy rule excludes.
- Adobe's own workaround for HEIC on Windows is to set the iPhone to send JPEG ("Transfer to Mac or PC: Automatic", or "Most Compatible"). This is the one route here that needs neither a purchase, a bundled decoder nor the cloud.

## For the map

- **#48** (what Trovolve does on a PC that cannot decode HEIC for free): the market norm is to point to the Store and the $0.99 extension, which Trovolve's own rule forbids requiring. Nothing seen gives a route that is free, local and has no bundled LGPL decoder, besides PCs whose maker installed the free OEM extension (#42) and the GPU (#50). Two possible inputs to #48 do not appear in the tickets: the iPhone's own JPEG (Transfer to Mac or PC, Most Compatible), and Trovolve's phone app working out each photo's measures on the phone with ImageIO and sending them with the backup, so that the computer need not decode the HEIC at all.
- **Zoner** is the one maker found that both relies on Windows' codecs and ships an HEVC decoder of its own, and it holds both pool licences. That is the "vendor who pays the pools" case that #41's limits allow, but it is a commercial SDK route, which #41 puts out of scope.
