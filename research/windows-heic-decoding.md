# What can Windows decode HEIC with, at no cost to the person?

Research for issue #42 (map #41). Gathered 2026-11-25. Facts are cited; where something is undocumented or uncertain it says so. Nothing here is legal advice: what the patent pools' and Microsoft's terms say is reported as they word it, for the lawyer to confirm (STEPS.md, Before selling).

## Short answer

- **Every Windows route to an iPhone's HEIC goes through an HEVC decoder.** WIC's HEIF codec (the free **HEIF Image Extension**, preinstalled on Windows 11) reads the container only; the picture inside needs **HEVC Video Extensions** as well. Microsoft's own support page tells people to get both, and prices them: HEIF free, HEVC **$0.99**.
- **The HEVC decoder comes in two editions with the same decoder:** **HEVC Video Extensions** (`9NMZLZ57R3T7`, package family `Microsoft.HEVCVideoExtensions`, $0.99, anyone can buy it) and **HEVC Video Extensions from Device Manufacturer** (`9N4WGH0Z6VHQ`, family `Microsoft.HEVCVideoExtension`, $0, preinstalled by PC makers in their Windows images). The Store's own catalogue offers the free edition with no Purchase or Browse action and no price: a person cannot get it unless the PC already holds its licence. So **an app cannot send a person to install the free edition**; it can only find out whether it is already there.
- **How many PCs have the free edition is not published anywhere.** It is in Microsoft's OEM deployment lab as a standard part of an OEM image, and the development PC (an OEM Windows 11 Home laptop) has it. A Windows 11 clean install provisions the HEIF, WebP and VP9 extensions but not HEVC. Retail, self-built and clean-installed PCs, virtual machines and N editions usually lack it.
- **GPU decoding without the extension is possible, and Chrome does it** (Direct3D 11 video decoding, "no need to install anything"), on most GPUs since about 2015–2016, the HEVC Main Still Picture profile included since Chrome 128. But the program must parse the HEVC bitstream itself and drive the GPU, so it is arguably an HEVC decoder of Trovolve's own in all but the pixel maths. Who holds the patent licence then is not settled by any public term. HEVC Advance's 2016 royalty-free software policy reportedly excluded "software that enables specialized hardware functionality".
- **No other system route avoids HEVC.** Shell thumbnails (`IShellItemImageFactory`, `IThumbnailProvider`) use the same codecs, and an iPhone HEIC's own thumbnail is HEVC too. The new Photos app (rolling out to Insiders from 2026-10-15) is reported to open HEIC without the extensions, but Microsoft has not said so, and nothing suggests other programs could use it.
- **Patent exposure of a program that only calls the system's decoder:** both pools license whoever sells a product that *incorporates* an HEVC decoder (HEVC Advance: "devices or software ... which items incorporate one or more HEVC Decoders"; VCL Advance, formerly Via LA: "devices implementing the technology"). Trovolve calling Windows' decoder incorporates none, and Microsoft's terms carry the HEVC Advance notice for its own software. On that reading Trovolve takes no licence, but the lawyer must confirm it.

**Free and nothing installed:** only on PCs that already have the Device Manufacturer edition (number unknown). **Free with an install:** the HEIF extension only, which is useless for HEIC without HEVC. **Costs:** HEVC Video Extensions, $0.99. **Free, nothing installed, any GPU since about 2016:** Direct3D 11 video decoding, at the cost of Trovolve writing an HEVC bitstream front end, with its licence question open.

| HEIF Image Extension | 9PMMSR1CGPWG | `Microsoft.HEIFImageExtension_8wekyb3d8bbwe` | Microsoft Windows | Free | yes | 2026-11-20 |
| HEVC Video Extensions | 9NMZLZ57R3T7 | `Microsoft.HEVCVideoExtensions_8wekyb3d8bbwe` | Microsoft Corporation | $0.99 | yes | 2026-10-30 |
| HEVC Video Extensions from Device Manufacturer | 9N4WGH0Z6VHQ | `Microsoft.HEVCVideoExtension_8wekyb3d8bbwe` | Microsoft Windows | $0 (no price shown) | **no** (Details, Fulfill, Redeem only) | 2026-10-30 |
| HEIF and HEVC Media Extensions (bundle) | 9NTLD6MSD8BM | (none) | Microsoft Corporation | $0.99 | yes | 2026-03-07 |

- The HEIF extension's description: "The HEVC Video Extensions package must be installed in order to view images stored in HEIF files that use the .heic, .hif or .heif file extensions." (Store catalogue, 9PMMSR1CGPWG.) Microsoft's support article says the same to people: "Buy both of these extensions ... You'll need both, even if you only plan on using photos", listing "HEIF Image Extensions (free)" and "HEVC Video Extensions ($0.99)". [S1]
- Both HEVC editions carry the same description word for word: hardware decoding on "Intel 7th Generation Core processor and newer GPU", and "for devices that don't have hardware support for HEVC videos, software support is provided". The free edition is not limited to PCs with HEVC hardware, as one forum answer claims [S9]. Both are for x86, x64 and Arm64, on Windows 10 1709 (16299) or later.
- The free edition's catalogue entry has a release date of 9998-12-30, which keeps it out of search and browse, and no Purchase action. The paid edition and the HEIF extension have Purchase, Browse and Curate. This is read from the catalogue's own fields. Microsoft does not document what they mean for a person, and community reports differ on whether the free edition can be got from a direct link [S9][S10]. The catalogue's fields give no way for a PC without the licence to acquire it.
- **Preinstalled:** Microsoft's OEM deployment lab for Windows 11 adds `Microsoft.HEVCVideoExtension_8wekyb3d8bbwe.x64.appx`, the free edition's family, with its licence file as a provisioned app [S4]. Reporting describes the edition as made for PC makers to preinstall, on the understanding that they pay the HEVC royalty [S10]. A Windows 11 (21H2) list of provisioned packages has `Microsoft.HEIFImageExtension`, `Microsoft.VP9VideoExtensions`, `Microsoft.WebpImageExtension` and `Microsoft.WebMediaExtensions`, and no HEVC [S5]. A 25H2 image log lists HEIF, Raw and AV1 extensions installed, again with no HEVC [S6].
- **On how many PCs:** no source gives a count or share. Not published by Microsoft or any PC maker found. On the development PC (an OEM Windows 11 Home laptop, 26H2) the installed packages are `Microsoft.HEVCVideoExtension` 2.5.48.0 (the free edition), `Microsoft.HEIFImageExtension` 1.2.65.0, AV1, VP9, WebP, Raw and MPEG-2 (`Get-AppxPackage`). Which PC makers include it, on which models and since when, is undocumented.
- **Windows on Arm:** both HEVC editions ship Arm64 builds (catalogue). Whether Snapdragon Copilot+ PCs come with the free edition was not found in any Microsoft or PC maker source.
- **N editions:** these lack Media Foundation until the Media Feature Pack is added [S11], so the HEVC extension cannot work there before it.
- **Windows 10:** support ended on 2025-10-14 [S12]. The extensions still list Windows 10 1709 as their minimum.
- **Changing?** The two HEVC editions were updated on 2026-10-30 and the HEIF extension on 2026-11-20, so both are maintained. Microsoft has announced no change to their pricing or availability. The new Photos app, rolling out from 2026-10-15, edits and saves HEIC in its original format [S7]. A Neowin report says it opens HEIC "without requiring any additional extensions", but Microsoft has not said how [S8]. If Photos carries its own decoder, nothing suggests WIC or other programs could use it.

## 2. May an app send a person to install the free edition, and Microsoft's terms on relying on either

- **Sending to the Store:** Microsoft documents `ms-windows-store://pdp/?ProductId=<id>` as "the recommended way to link to a specific product" [S13]. Microsoft's HEIF codec page tells app developers: "The HEVC and AV1 codecs might not be available on all PCs. You can download those codecs from the Microsoft Store. To check whether HEVC or AV1 are installed, you can use the MFTEnumEx function" [S3]. So pointing a person to the Store is Microsoft's own pattern.
- **The free edition:** as above, the catalogue gives a person without its licence no way to get it, so a link would show a page with nothing to buy (undocumented behaviour, not tried). **Trovolve can only detect it:** its package family (`Microsoft.HEVCVideoExtension`, no "s") tells it apart from the paid one (`Microsoft.HEVCVideoExtensions`). Either one decodes, and WIC does not care which.
- **Relying on either:** both are described as extending Windows "to play HEVC videos in any video app", and WIC's HEIF codec is documented for apps to use [S3]. Neither listing has licence terms of its own: both point to the Microsoft Services Agreement, whose Standard Application License Terms apply. These bind the person, not a program calling the codec, and forbid working "around any technical limitations in the application" [S14]. The agreement carries Access Advance's notice: "IF INCLUDED, THE H.265/HEVC TECHNOLOGY IN THIS SOFTWARE IS COVERED BY ONE OR MORE CLAIMS OF THE HEVC PATENTS ... DEPENDING ON HOW YOU OBTAINED THE SOFTWARE, THIS PRODUCT MAY BE LICENSED UNDER THE HEVC ADVANCE PATENT PORTFOLIO." [S14] No Microsoft term was found that restricts which programs may call the system's HEVC or HEIF decoders. None was found that grants a third-party app anything either.
- **Other vendors:** CopyTrans HEIC for Windows installs a WIC codec of its own, free for personal use only. Businesses, schools and governments need a paid licence [S15]. Whether it pays the HEVC pools is not stated. It is a third-party decoder, so it falls outside the map's limits unless the vendor's licensing is confirmed.

## 3. Decoding through the GPU without the extension

- **Media Foundation's HEVC decoder is the extension.** Microsoft's reference for the H.265 decoder MFT gives its DLLs as `hevcdecoder.dll` and `hevcdecoder_store.dll`. It decodes the Main, Main Still Picture and Main10 profiles, uses DX11/DX12 DXVA where the hardware has it and software otherwise, and is limited to 4096 × 2304 [S2]. On the development PC the graphics drivers (Intel and NVIDIA) register only HEVC *encoder* MFTs system-wide, no decoder (`HKLM\SOFTWARE\Classes\MediaFoundation\Transforms`). Without the extension, Media Foundation and so WIC's HEIF codec have no HEVC decoder there. Whether the HEIF extension would use a non-Microsoft HEVC decoder MFT if one were registered is undocumented.
- **Direct3D 11/12 video decoding is part of Windows and the driver, not the extension.** The decoder profiles (`D3D11_DECODER_PROFILE_HEVC_VLD_MAIN`, Main10) are listed by `ID3D11VideoDevice::GetVideoDecoderProfile` [S16]. Microsoft publishes the DXVA HEVC specification, which covers only off-host VLD profiles, meaning the GPU does the entropy decoding and reconstruction [S16]. The program must still parse the parameter sets and slice headers and fill DXVA's picture parameters.
- **Chrome does exactly this:** "Chrome uses D3D11VideoDecoder to call D3D11VA (no need to install anything)", while Edge calls the MFT and needs the extension. It is hardware only, on Windows 8 and later, on Intel HD 4400/HD 515, NVIDIA GT 635/GTX 645, AMD RX 460, AMD Radeon R7/Vega M and Qualcomm Adreno 618 or later. It handles "HEVC Main Still Picture Profile ... for Windows" from Chrome 128, up to 8192 × 8192 [S17]. Chrome's support of HEVC since 107 is hardware only [S18].
- **What it would mean for Trovolve:** an iPhone HEIC is a grid of HEVC-coded tiles (to be confirmed by #43). Each tile would be parsed and sent to the GPU, then assembled and colour-converted by Trovolve. That is an HEVC bitstream front end of Trovolve's own (Chromium has its own `h265_parser`), though no reconstruction is done in software. It is no help on a PC whose GPU lacks HEVC decoding, or in a virtual machine or remote session. Whether this counts as the "HEVC decoder of Trovolve's own" the map forbids is Jamie's call.
- **Who holds the patent licence then:** not stated in any public term. HEVC Advance licenses "parties that Sell substantially completed devices or software ... which items incorporate one or more HEVC Decoders", and focuses on "OEM's and other parties that customarily handle licensing obligations" [S19]. A PC maker pays per device for the decoder in its hardware. Whether software that drives that hardware is a further "HEVC Decoder" is for the lawyer. HEVC Advance's 2016 royalty-free software policy covered "application layer software ... fully executed in software on a general purpose CPU" [S20], and is reported to exclude "software that enables specialized hardware functionality" and operating systems [S21]. The July 2025 programme overview no longer mentions that policy [S19], and whether it still stands was not found.

## 4. Other routes already on the system

- **Shell thumbnails** (`IShellItemImageFactory::GetImage`, the thumbnail cache, `IThumbnailProvider`): Explorer's HEIC thumbnails come from the HEIF extension, which needs the HEVC decoder like any other reader. An iPhone HEIC's embedded thumbnail is also an HEVC-coded item, so nothing on the system shows a HEIC without HEVC. The shell API is documented and public, so a program may use it, but its pictures are scaled thumbnails. They are no better than WIC's own output, and the same codec makes them. Not measured here.
- **The Photos app:** see section 1. An app-private decoder, if that is what it is, is not reachable by other programs through any documented API.
- **OneDrive:** Microsoft suggests uploading HEIC to OneDrive to convert it [S1]. That sends photos off the device, which Trovolve's rules forbid.
- **The iPhone's own conversion:** Microsoft notes that phones can convert to JPEG on transfer [S1]. That is the person's choice before Trovolve sees the files, not a decoder.

## 5. Patent exposure of a program that only calls the system's decoder

As the pools' own words state it, for the lawyer to confirm:

- **HEVC Advance (Access Advance), July 2025 overview:** "We license HEVC Decoders and HEVC Encoders in Consumer HEVC Products, whether made available in devices, software or through Cloud Based Services." Licences go to "Parties that Sell substantially completed devices or software ... which items incorporate one or more HEVC Decoders and/or HEVC Encoders". "Royalties are due for HEVC Software Sold separately from a device". The pool "reserves the right to seek licenses from any party infringing" but its "primary focus" is branded products and OEMs [S19]. Trovolve calling Windows' decoder incorporates none. Microsoft and the PC maker hold the licence for the decoder they ship (Microsoft's terms carry HEVC Advance's notice [S14]).
- **VCL Advance (formerly Via LA, acquired by Access Advance on 2025-12-15)** [S22]: "the HEVC/VVC portfolio license extends to devices implementing the technology", sold to end users by the brand owner; $0 for the first 100,000 units, then $0.30 a unit in Region 1 and $0.20 in Region 2, capped at $30,000,000 a year per enterprise [S23].
- **Patent holders outside both pools** (Wikipedia notes the merger "doesn't resolve the licensing situation" [S24]) are not covered by any pool term. Their exposure for a calling program is unknown.
- **Reading:** a program that ships no HEVC decoder and calls the one Windows provides incorporates no "HEVC Decoder" in either pool's terms. The licence sits with whoever ships the decoder (Microsoft, the PC maker). This is the plain reading of the pools' summaries, not their full licence agreements, which were not read. The Direct3D route (section 3) is less clear.

## What this means for the map

- **#48 is not hypothetical:** on any PC without the Device Manufacturer edition, the only Microsoft route costs $0.99. Trovolve cannot send a person to the free edition. The current branch's message ("Install HEVC Video Extensions from the Microsoft Store …") sends people to a paid product, against the map's limit that nothing must be bought.
- **Detection is cheap:** the two package families tell the editions apart. `MFTEnumEx` for an HEVC decoder, or a trial decode through WIC, says whether HEIC can be read at all.
- **A possible new ticket:** whether the Direct3D 11 route (Chrome's) is within "no HEVC decoder of Trovolve's own", what it costs to build, and the lawyer's view of software driving the GPU's decoder.
