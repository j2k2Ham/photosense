# Support

{{name}} has a guide built in: choose Help in the menu, or in Settings on a phone or tablet. It explains every screen, and its search finds what you are looking for. Support, in the same place, gathers these questions, the errors {{name}} has met, and a way to write to us.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do the files I delete go?

On a computer, into a folder named `_Trovolve_Removed`, inside the folder they came from, keeping their place within it. Nothing is erased. Undo, in the message that appears, puts them back.

On a phone or tablet, into Recently Deleted in Photos (the bin on Android), where they wait for 30 days. {{name}} cannot take them back out itself: to bring one back, open it there in Photos or your gallery app.

### How do I erase them for good?

On a computer, choose **Move to Recycle Bin** in the menu (**Move to Trash** on a Mac). {{name}} says how many files are waiting and how much space they take, and asks before anything goes. They can be got back from the Recycle Bin until it is emptied. A folder on a drive with no Recycle Bin, such as a memory card or a network share, is erased for good, and {{name}} names it in red before it asks.

On a phone or tablet, empty Recently Deleted in Photos, or the bin in your gallery app.

### Can I undo a move?

Yes. **Recently moved**, in Organize (and in Settings on a phone or tablet), lists what can still be undone, and **Undo** puts the files back where they were, under the names they had. A photo deleted on a phone or tablet is not undone in {{name}}: bring it back from Recently Deleted or the bin.

### What are Clean up's categories?

Large videos, screenshots, bursts, blurry shots, and what messaging apps such as WhatsApp and Telegram saved. On a computer, files deleted from them move to `_Trovolve_Removed`, and Undo puts them back. On a phone or tablet they go to Recently Deleted (the bin on Android) for 30 days.

### Does {{name}} upload my photos?

No. {{name}} runs on your own device, and nothing is uploaded: your photos, and everything {{name}} learns about them, stay there. They go only where you send them: to your own computer, or to the cloud storage and servers you connect. {{name}} has no ads, and does not track what is in your photos. See the privacy policy.

### Why does {{name}} not show all my photos on my iPhone or iPad?

When {{name}} first asked to see your photos, you may have chosen to allow only some. The scan screen then says so: **Choose more photos** opens the phone's own picker, and **Open Settings** goes to where all of them can be allowed, under Privacy & Security, then Photos, then {{name}}.

### Why can {{name}} not read the photos from my iPhone on Windows?

iPhones save photos as HEIC. Windows reads HEIC once two free extensions from the Microsoft Store are installed: HEIF Image Extensions and HEVC Video Extensions. {{name}} uses Windows' own decoder, so it reads them as soon as Windows can; the scan's log says which extension is missing. A Mac reads HEIC as it is.

### What is the difference between duplicates and similar shots?

Duplicates are copies of the same file. Similar shots are different pictures that look alike, such as a burst taken a moment apart. {{name}} suggests the best of each set to keep, and you decide.

### How do I remove {{name}} and everything it keeps?

On an iPhone or iPad, delete the app. On a computer, delete the program and {{name}}'s data folder for your user account. Files you removed but have not erased are still in the `_Trovolve_Removed` folders.

## Writing to us

If the guide does not answer your question, write to {{supportEmail}}. **Email support**, in Support, starts the message with the version of {{name}} and your system already in it. To help us help you, please say:

- which device and system you use (for example, iPhone 17 with iOS 26, or Windows 11);
- the version of {{name}}, shown under About in the menu, or in Settings on a phone or tablet;
- what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead.

Please do not send photos unless we ask for one to look into a problem.
